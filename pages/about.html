// About Me

I'm Abd Ulrahman Mardini (Jaden), a second-year Computer Science student at the University of Guelph with a focus on cybersecurity and a background in Computer Engineering from Conestoga College.

I've always been curious about how technology works, but even more interested in what happens when it doesn't. Whether I'm investigating a security vulnerability, building a software application, or experimenting with network protocols, I enjoy taking things apart, understanding the logic behind them, and figuring out how to make them better.

Over the past five years, that curiosity has turned into more than 35 software and cybersecurity projects spanning vulnerability scanning, network security, data engineering, automation, and IoT. Some of my work includes developing a Python-based web vulnerability scanner, building a custom CTF platform, experimenting with UDP packet transmission, and creating applications that turn complex data into meaningful insights. I use GitHub not just to showcase finished projects, but to document the process of learning, experimenting, making mistakes, and improving.

My experience also extends beyond personal projects. At LinePal, I performed security assessments that identified 14 confirmed vulnerabilities. At ClariNexus, I worked on data pipelines and application development, implementing improvements that reduced data anomalies by over 70%. These experiences showed me how much attention to detail, communication, and practical problem-solving matter when building reliable systems.

One of my proudest accomplishments was founding Conestoga College's first cybersecurity club. As president, I led a team of four executives, helped grow a community of over 80 students, and organized hands-on workshops covering Capture the Flag challenges, Kali Linux, cryptography, and offensive and defensive security. What started as an idea became a place where students could learn from each other, experiment, and explore cybersecurity outside the classroom.

Today, I'm continuing to develop my skills in security operations, software engineering, and threat detection. My long-term goal is to build a career in cybersecurity where I can help organizations understand threats, strengthen their defenses, and create technology people can trust.

For me, the most rewarding part of technology isn't just getting something to work. It's understanding why it works, where it might fail, and how to make it better.
